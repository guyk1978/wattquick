EV owners hear “fewer moving parts” and picture zero maintenance. Tires did not get that memo. Many drivers replace rubber **thousands of kilometers earlier** than on their last gas car—not because EVs are defective, but because the load case changed.

## High torque at zero RPM

ICE engines build torque with RPM; many EVs deliver peak twist **immediately**. Traction control masks wheelspin, but the contact patch still sees shear during:

- Hard launches from traffic lights  
- Highway on-ramps with full pedal  
- Low-speed parking-lot maneuvers on cold rubber  

Torque does not wear tires only when you hear squeal. Micro-slip accelerates shoulder wear, especially on front tires in FWD layouts and on wide OEM sizes.

### Weight from the battery pack

A 75 kWh crossover can outweigh its gas twin by hundreds of kilograms. More mass means:

- Higher contact patch load  
- More heat in the carcass at highway speed  
- Faster wear when combined with under-inflation  

Mass also affects **suspension geometry** over bumps—misalignment shows up as feathered edges within ten thousand kilometers.

## Compounds tuned for EVs, not maximum miles

Low rolling resistance (LRR) tires reduce Wh/mi and cabin noise. Tradeoffs can include:

- Softer tread for grip on wet pavement with heavy vehicles  
- Narrower contact shapes optimized for aero  
- OEM specs that prioritize range ratings over 80,000 km life  

Replacing with cheapest all-seasons may **increase** wear or noise if load index and EV ratings are wrong—match placard speed/load.

## Tire pressure: the free maintenance lever

Quiet cabins hide vibration from soft tires. EVs need correct PSI because:

- Under-inflation increases flex heat → faster wear and range loss  
- Over-inflation reduces grip and can center-wear the tread  
- Temperature swings (garage to summer road) change PSI by 10+ kPa  

Check cold PSI monthly; add 3–5 PSI only if OEM allows for heavy loads. Use the **door jamb label**, not random gas-station defaults.

### Rotation and regen bias

Follow OEM rotation intervals. One-pedal driving can shift axle loading—some fleets see rear tires die first on RWD performance EVs, fronts on heavy FWD commuters. Uneven wear is a alignment or pressure problem until proven otherwise.
