Are your solar collectors working at peak performance? Calculate your solar water heater efficiency and unlock the secrets to truly free hot water.

Solar domestic hot water is not magic—it is heat transfer with a receipt. Sunlight hits an aperture, fluid or a jacket transfers energy into storage, and you draw hot water hours later. When performance slips, owners blame the tank before they measure **whether intercepted solar actually became sensible heat in the store**. That gap is thermal efficiency, and it is measurable with field temperatures, not guesswork.

## Why thermal efficiency is the right metric

Photovoltaic systems report kWh at the inverter. Solar thermal systems report comfort at the shower—and frustration when the top element runs too often. Efficiency answers a sharper question:

> Of the solar energy that landed on my collector, how much showed up as heated water?

Financial payback and carbon benefit both scale with that ratio. A oversized tank with undersized aperture looks “fine” in summer and fails in shoulder seasons.

## The physics in plain language

Heating water is specific heat arithmetic:

```
Water kWh ≈ liters × 4.186 × ΔT ÷ 3600
Incident kWh ≈ collector m² × sun hours × ~0.75 kW/m²
Efficiency % ≈ water kWh ÷ incident kWh (≤ 100%)
```

The calculator compares the same water kWh to **resistance element cost** at your retail rate—useful when backup electricity still dominates the bill.

### What good looks like on site

| Signal | Healthy range | Investigate if… |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| Efficiency vs. incident | Often 35–55% real-world | &lt; 25% on clear days |
| ΔT per sun hour | Stable season curve | Collapses after 2–3 years |
| Backup element hours | Low in summer | Rises while sun hours stable |

## Diagnose before you replace hardware

1. **Scale** on heat exchangers and potable coils acts like insulation you cannot see.  
2. **Shading** from new trees or HVAC equipment cuts aperture energy without tripping alarms.  
3. **Stagnation** in summer overheats glycol loops and accelerates degradation.  
4. **Tank stratification**—a single top sensor hides a cold bottom layer.  

Log morning inlet and afternoon outlet temperatures on clear days; rerun the calculator monthly to catch drift.
