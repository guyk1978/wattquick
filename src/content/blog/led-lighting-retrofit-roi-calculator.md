A whole-home LED retrofit is one of the few upgrades you can finish in a weekend and verify on next month's bill. The physics is trivial—same lumens, fewer watts—but the buying decision is not: color temperature, dimmer compatibility, enclosed fixture ratings, and rebate forms all change ROI.

## Lumen matching vs watt chasing

Old habits equated "60 W bulb" with brightness. LEDs advertise lumens and efficacy (lm/W). Match target lumens per room function—kitchen task higher, hallway lower—then pick the lowest watt label that hits it with quality CRI (80+ for living spaces, 90+ where color matters).

### Halogen and incandescent baseline

A 60 W incandescent near 800 lm at roughly 13 lm/W. A 9 W LED at 800 lm is near 90 lm/W—seven× less power for the same light if drivers are honest.

## Bill impact and payback

Multiply saved watts by hours on × rate. Closets matter less than kitchen cans on three hours nightly. Outdoor security LEDs on dusk-to-dawn timers dominate some homes.

### Dimming and lifetime

Cheap LEDs on old triac dimmers flicker; buy dimmer-rated SKUs or replace switches. Quality chips last 15,000–50,000 hours—payback often under two years on heavy-use fixtures before rebates.

## Implementation order

1. Count bulbs and note base types (A19, BR30, GU10)
2. Heavy-use rooms first
3. Utility rebate portal pre-check
4. Dispose old CFLs properly (mercury)

## Beyond payback

Lower waste heat reduces summer cooling load slightly—secondary savings on AC-heavy climates. Fewer ladder trips because you are not relamping incandescents every year.

LED retrofits are spreadsheet-friendly efficiency. Match lumens, cut watts, run the payback with your real rates and hours—then buy once instead of experimenting aisle by aisle.
